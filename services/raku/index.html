  • What is Raku? Raku is a low-firing process originally inspired by traditional Japanese techniques, particularly those associated with the Japanese tea ceremony and Buddhist philosophy. Western-style raku typically involves removing pottery from the kiln at bright red heat and placing it into containers filled with combustible materials. As these materials ignite, the containers are sealed, creating an intense reduction atmosphere that alters the colors of glazes and clay bodies. The rapid cooling and drastic thermal shock inherent to the process often result in crackling—deliberate cracks that add texture and visual interest to the piece. While the original Japanese raku was shaped by spiritual and cultural influences, the Western adaptation focuses on the dramatic effects produced by this high-risk, low-firing technique.
